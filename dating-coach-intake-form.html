The 8 fields

FIELD 1

What name should I call you?

Type: Short text · Required: Yes · Field key: preferred_name

Marina uses the client’s preferred name on the call and in the Partner Blueprint. Some people go by a nickname or middle name — this confirms.
FIELD 2

What is the relationship outcome you actually want? In your own words — not in coaching language.

Type: Long text · Required: Yes · Min length: 50 chars · Field key: desired_outcome

Placeholder: “A real partner. Marriage. Kids. Travel together. Someone I can build with...”

Anchors the call. Lets Marina see what success looks like for THIS client — not a generic version. The 50-char minimum forces a real answer.
FIELD 3

What pattern keeps repeating, that you’re tired of?

Type: Long text · Required: Yes · Min length: 50 chars · Field key: pattern

Placeholder: “I keep falling for the same wrong type. They start strong, go cold, come back. I do this every 18 months.”

The single most important field for the Blueprint. This is the “diagnosis” section’s raw material.
FIELD 4

What have you tried so far that hasn’t worked?

Type: Long text · Required: Yes · Min length: 30 chars · Field key: tried_already

Placeholder: “Therapy, dating apps, books, taking breaks, more therapy...”

Saves Marina from suggesting things they’ve done. Also tells her what kind of work the client respects.
FIELD 5

Looking 90 days from now — what would have to be true for this Session to feel worth it?

Type: Long text · Required: Yes · Min length: 30 chars · Field key: success_test

Placeholder: “I’d know my pattern, I’d have a clear next move, I’d feel less stuck...”

The client’s own definition of success becomes the action plan’s benchmark. Marina maps the 30-day plan against this answer specifically.
FIELD 6

Is there anything you’d rather not discuss on the call?

Type: Long text · Required: No (optional) · Field key: off_limits

Placeholder: “Specifics of the divorce. Anything about my kids by name. The financial stuff with my ex.”

Critical boundary-setter. Some clients have topics that are too raw or simply not relevant. Knowing in advance keeps Marina from accidentally walking into them.
FIELD 7

What’s the one question you most want me to answer on the call?

Type: Long text · Required: Yes · Min length: 30 chars · Field key: main_question

Placeholder: “Why does this keep happening to me?” / “Should I leave or stay?” / “Am I the problem?”

Forces clarity. Even if the call goes elsewhere, Marina makes sure to land this one before the hour ends.
FIELD 8

Anything else you want me to know before we meet?

Type: Long text · Required: No (optional) · Field key: extras

Placeholder: Anything — context, history, a feeling, a worry. Whatever helps me show up prepared.

The catch-all. Sometimes the most useful information shows up here.

Where this goes in the funnel

  1. Client pays $497 via Stripe.
  2. Booking confirmation email fires (the first file in this set).
  3. Booking email links to this intake form.
  4. Client fills out the 7 questions, hits submit.
  5. Tally sends the answers to Marina via email (or to a Notion database, depending on her preference).
  6. Marina reads them before the call.
  7. Marina runs the call already knowing the situation.
  8. 48 business hours after the call: Partner Blueprint goes out (the third file in this set), structured around the answers in Fields 2-7.

Build checklist

  1. Sign in to Tally (same account as the marketing quiz).
  2. Create a new form. Name: Pre-Session Intake — Clarity Session.
  3. Add the 8 fields exactly as spec’d above. Set the field keys for webhook compatibility.
  4. Style the form: cream background, deep green text, gold accent on buttons. Same brand as everything else.
  5. URL slug: clarity-intake. Full URL: https://tally.so/r/clarity-intake.
  6. Add the URL to the booking-email template (replaces {{ intake_form_link }}).
  7. Set the form to email submissions to Marina’s inbox (Tally Settings → Notifications).
  8. Test by submitting yourself with fake answers.
