Part 1 — 3 sample bios to react to

Most people freeze at “write your bio.” It’s easier to react to something. Here are 3 different angles. Marina picks the one closest to her, and we customize from there. None of these are committed — they’re prompts for her own voice.

Sample A — The transformation arc

“I was where you are now”

For Marina, if her own dating story is part of why she does this work and she’s comfortable being slightly personal.

For most of my adult life, I was the friend everyone called for relationship advice — while quietly making the same mistakes in my own dating life. I dated emotionally unavailable men. I overstayed in relationships that didn’t fit. I called it patience.

It took years to learn what I now teach in 90 days: that the work isn’t about meeting different people, it’s about changing what your body trusts. Once that shifts, the right people don’t feel boring — they feel like ground.

Now I work with people who are successful in every other area of their life and stuck in this one. I help them see the pattern that’s costing them, get clear on what they actually want, and become the person the right partner will recognize.

Sample B — The professional path

“Years of doing this work, now formalized”

For Marina, if she has a background (psychology, coaching certification, social work, or simply “decades of being the one people came to”) and wants to lead with credibility.

I’ve spent [years/most of my career] working with people on the parts of their lives that don’t respond to a checklist. Dating, in particular, is where high-functioning people get most stuck — because it’s the one place their usual tools (planning, optimization, sheer effort) stop working.

I built the 4-P Method — Presence, Picture, Presentation, Progression — out of years of watching the same patterns repeat across hundreds of conversations. It’s the framework I use with every client.

My work is direct, calm, and grounded in what actually changes outcomes — not what sounds good in a feed. I don’t do scripts, I don’t do hype, and I don’t do therapy. I help you see clearly so you can choose well.

Sample C — The observer / direct voice

“Here’s what I do, plainly”

For Marina, if she’d rather skip the personal narrative and lead with what the work actually is. Cleanest option for clients who don’t care about her story.

I coach the inner game of dating — for people who’ve already done the outer work. Career. Money. Health. Friendships. The dating part is what’s left.

My clients are usually 32 to 55, single or divorced, looking for a long-term partner. They’ve tried the apps, the setups, the optimization. None of it worked because none of it was the actual problem.

The actual problem is almost always one of four things: the inner state you bring, the clarity you don’t have on what you want, the way you show up, or how you let things move forward. I help you see which of the four is yours, and what to do about it.

I’m direct. I don’t over-explain. The work either lands or it doesn’t — and you’ll know which by the end of the first session.

Marina: if none of these feel quite right but the closest is Sample A, tell me “A, but warmer” or “A, less personal.” If you want to combine two (e.g. “the credibility of B with the directness of C”), tell me. I’ll write the final version once I know your direction.

Part 2 — 6 questions to answer (5 minutes)

Whichever sample Marina picks, the final bio uses her real specifics. These 6 questions extract them.

1
Where do you live?

City, region, or simply “the East Coast.” Adds geographic grounding. Optional — skip if she’d rather not say.

2
One specific personal detail you’re comfortable sharing.

A hobby, a daily ritual, a place you go every weekend, a thing you make. One sentence. Humanizes you without giving up privacy. Examples: “I run before sunrise most mornings.” / “I’m always within reach of a good cup of coffee and a notebook.” / “I cook the same Sunday dinner I’ve been making for 20 years.”

3
Why this work?

1-2 sentences. Why dating coaching specifically — not therapy, not life coaching, not matchmaking. What pulled you into this niche?

4
Any formal training, certifications, or background that’s relevant?

Honest answer. If yes, list. If no, that’s also fine — we’ll lean on Sample A or C. Examples: “ICF coaching certification, 2022.” / “15 years working in [field] before this.” / “I’m self-taught from a decade of being the person everyone came to.”

5
Your own dating story in one sentence (only if you’re willing).

This is optional but powerful. People trust coaches who’ve done the work themselves. One line. Examples: “I’ve been divorced once and I know what it’s like to start over in your forties.” / “I dated emotionally unavailable men for ten years before I learned the pattern was mine to break.” / Skip entirely if uncomfortable.

6
One specific kind of client you’re NOT a good fit for.

Counter-intuitive but powerful. Filtering out the wrong people makes the right ones lean in harder. Examples: “I don’t work with people who want pickup tactics or scripts.” / “I’m not for people looking for someone to blame their last relationship on.” / “I’m not for people who want a guarantee of an outcome.”

Part 3 — The photo

One photo. Square or 3:4 portrait. At least 600×600 pixels. Doesn’t need to be expensive — a recent phone photo from a friend in good natural light is often better than a corporate headshot.

DO

  • Recent (within 1-2 years)
  • Just Marina — not a group shot
  • Natural light, neutral or warm background
  • Warm but grounded expression — not over-polished
  • Clothes Marina would actually wear to coach in
  • Eye contact with the camera

DON’T

  • Heavy filters or beauty apps
  • Old photos that don’t look like Marina now
  • Hyper-stylized fashion shoots
  • Pets or kids in the frame
  • Sunglasses
  • Group photos cropped down

Cheap and effective: ask a friend with a recent iPhone. Stand near a window with soft natural light. Take 30 photos. Pick the one where Marina looks like she’s about to say something true. Done.

If you want to invest: a $200–400 headshot session with a local portrait photographer (search “personal-brand photographer [city]”) gets professional results and you’ll have 5–10 photos to use across the website, social, and future content for a year.
