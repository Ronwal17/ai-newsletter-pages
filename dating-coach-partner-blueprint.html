01What I saw

[2-3 sentence summary of the pattern Marina noticed during the call. Use the client’s own words where possible. Open with what they came in saying, name the deeper thing underneath. 02What it’s costing you

[1-2 sentences naming the specific cost — in time, in self-worth, in years. Be direct, not dramatic. Example: “Eighteen-month cycles since the divorce. Three different faces, one repeating story. The cost isn’t the relationships ending. It’s that each ending leaves the wiring stronger.”]

03The 4-P diagnosis

Of the four pieces, here’s where you are right now:

Presence: [1 sentence on inner state. Steady? Anxious? Where’s the leak?]

Picture: [1 sentence on clarity of what they want. Clear? Vague? Defensive?]

Presentation: [1 sentence on how they show up. Aligned with what they want? Not yet?]

Progression: [1 sentence on how they handle the move from connection to relationship. Hold? Pull? Escape?]

The one to fix first: [Name which P. Almost always one of them is the bottleneck. Don’t try to fix all four at once.]

04The settling test, applied to you

You’re going to date someone in the next 90 days. Maybe sooner. Use this question to know whether to keep going or move on:

If this person never improved — same habits, same effort, same level — would I still want this life with them? Not tolerate. Want.

If the answer is no, you’re settling. If the answer is yes, you’re being realistic. [Optional: one sentence applying this directly to the client’s situation if relevant. Example: “Apply this to the next time he comes back charming. The answer will be obvious.”]

05The next 30 days

Five actions, in order. Don’t skip ahead. The order is the work.

  1. [Action 1 — usually inner-state work. Example: “For 30 days, no apps, no DMs after 9pm. Use the time to rebuild your nervous system before introducing new data.”]
  2. [Action 2 — usually clarity work. Example: “Write down what you actually want. Not what you should want. Use a notebook, not your phone. Take a full week.”]
  3. [Action 3 — usually about pattern recognition. Example: “Make a list of the last 3 men. For each, write the moment you knew it wasn’t right. That’s your radar — you’ve been ignoring it.”]
  4. [Action 4 — usually about how they show up. Example: “If you’re going back to the apps in week 4, redo your photos and rewrite the bio with what you wrote in step 2. Honest, not optimized.”]
  5. [Action 5 — usually a progression rule. Example: “On every date in week 4, ask yourself the settling-test question by the end. If the answer is no, end it cleanly within 7 days.”]

06What to watch for

The old pattern doesn’t leave on its own. Here’s how to know it’s pulling you back:

  • [Warning sign 1 — specific to the client’s pattern. Example: “You start telling yourself ‘he’s just stressed’ or ‘she’s just busy’ when the effort drops.”]
  • [Warning sign 2. Example: “You’re excited by butterflies again. Remember: butterflies are anxiety dressed up as romance. Calm is what you’re looking for.”]
  • [Warning sign 3. Example: “You skip step 1 because ‘you’re fine.’ You’re not. The skipping is the symptom.”]

07One question to sit with

[A single, sharp question for the client to carry into the next 30 days. Should be uncomfortable but answerable. Examples: “What would have to be true for you to believe you deserve a calm love?” / “If you trusted yourself completely, what would you do this week?” / “What part of you is afraid the right person will actually show up?”]

Don’t answer it now. Sit with it. The answer will come on its own — usually somewhere unexpected.

How to fill this out for each client

  1. Make a copy. File → Make a copy (in Google Docs version), or duplicate the HTML file with a new name like blueprint-2026-05-15-jane.html.
  2. Update the header. Replace [CLIENT NAME] and [SESSION DATE].
  3. Replace each placeholder in order. Sections 1-7. Don’t skip; don’t reorder.
  4. Read it out loud once. If anything feels generic or could apply to anyone, rewrite it. The whole point is that it sounds like it was written for THIS person.
  5. Export to PDF. File → Print → Save as PDF. Filename: Partner-Blueprint-[ClientName]-[Date].pdf.
  6. Email the PDF to the client with a 2-line note: “Here’s your Blueprint. Read it once today, once in a week. Reply if anything doesn’t land. — Marina”

Time budget per Blueprint

  • Reading the intake form again + reviewing call notes: 10 min
  • Filling in sections 1-3 (what you saw, cost, 4-P): 15 min
  • Filling in sections 4-7 (settling test, action plan, watchouts, sit-with question): 15 min
  • Read aloud, polish, export: 10 min
  • Total per client: ~50 minutes within 48 business hours of the call.

Voice rules for filling in placeholders

  • Use the client’s actual words from the intake or the call wherever possible — never paste generic coaching language.
  • Be specific. Generic = “you have an avoidance pattern.” Specific = “by week 6 you start finding reasons.”
  • Marina’s voice rules apply: dismiss self-blame, explain mechanism, no therapy-speak, no hype, no woo.
  • End every section with a shift — never just a description.
  • If something is hard, lead it with “honey” once. Only once.
