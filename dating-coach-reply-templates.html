Inbox Reply Templates

Five reusable responses for the most common replies prospects send. Marina customizes each one in 30 seconds before hitting send. The voice stays Marina’s — the specifics flex to the person.

Rule of thumb: always read the prospect’s reply twice before picking a template. The wrong template feels generic; the right one feels like a continuation of the conversation. Never copy-paste verbatim — the first sentence of every reply must use one specific word from their message.

Template 1 — Curious / wants to know more

When to use: they replied with curiosity, a follow-up question, or “this resonated — tell me more.” They’re engaged but not yet ready to commit. They’re asking for one more conversation.

The reply

[Name],

Glad it landed. The thing about [their specific phrase] is that it usually points to one of two patterns — and which one it is changes the entire next move.

If you want to figure out which is yours, the fastest way is the Clarity Session. One hour on Zoom, $497, and you walk out with a written Partner Blueprint — the actual pattern named, the next 30 days mapped, in your inbox within 48 hours.

If that’s a yes, here’s the link: [Stripe link].

If you’d rather think about it — that’s also fine. Tell me what’s holding you back and I’ll either answer it or tell you honestly that we’re not the right fit yet.

— Marina

What to customize: the bracketed phrase. Pick one specific thing they said that genuinely captures their pattern (not a generic word like “dating” — their actual word: “I get cold feet,” “I keep settling,” “I feel invisible”).

Template 2 — In pain / vulnerable / opened up

When to use: they shared something heavy — a divorce, a betrayal, deep loneliness, fear they’ll never find someone. They’re not ready for a sales pitch. They need to feel heard first.

The reply

[Name],

Thank you for sending that. What you’re describing is a real pain — and it’s the kind that doesn’t lift just because someone reads it.

I want to say two things.

First — you’re not behind. The story you’re telling yourself about [time / age / what you should have by now] is loud, but it’s not a fact. It’s a cracked self-image, and that’s the thing that has to change first. Everything else follows from there.

Second — if you want to do real work on this, the Clarity Session is where it starts. One hour, just us. We name the pattern, we map the next 30 days, you walk out with a written Partner Blueprint. $497, one-time. Link: [Stripe link].

If you’d rather just stay on this list and read — that’s also okay. Reply to any email. I’ll be here when you’re ready.

— Marina

What to customize: the bracketed time/age phrase. Use their words. Important: if they mention anything clinical (suicide, severe depression, abuse) — do NOT use this template. Use Template 5 instead and refer to a licensed therapist.

Template 3 — Ready to work / asks how to book

When to use: they directly asked about working together, pricing, or booking. They’ve made the decision. Don’t over-explain — deliver the link and confirm.

The reply

[Name],

Yes — let’s do it.

The Clarity Session is one hour on Zoom. Before our call I’ll send you a short pre-session form (5 minutes) so I walk in already knowing what you’re working with. We use the hour for the real work.

Within 48 business hours after the call, you’ll get your written Partner Blueprint — the pattern we identified, the one shift to focus on, and the 30-day action plan.

$497, one-time. Pay here and you’ll get a calendar link to book the time: [Stripe link].

Looking forward to it.

— Marina

What to customize: almost nothing. This one is meant to be efficient. Just the name and the Stripe link.

Template 4 — Not ready yet

When to use: they replied something like “not now,” “I need to think about it,” “I’m not in a place to commit,” or they ghosted after Email 4. The right move: zero pressure, leave the door open, stay generous.

The reply

[Name],

That makes sense. Don’t force it.

Coaching only works when the person sitting across from me actually wants to do the work — and the timing of that is something only you can feel. Pushing it doesn’t make it arrive faster.

Stay on this list. I send something when I have something worth sending. When the readiness shows up, you’ll know — and you can reply to any email and we’ll pick this back up.

In the meantime: the question I’d ask you, with no expectation of an answer, is — what would have to be true for you to know it’s time? That’s usually a more useful question than “am I ready.”

— Marina

What to customize: the name. Otherwise leave it. The point is to be generous, no pressure, no follow-up DM. They’ll come back when they’re ready — or they won’t. Either is fine.

Template 5 — Asked something off-topic or out of scope

When to use: their question is something the AI / system can’t handle — infidelity in a current relationship, polyamory, sexual issues, child custody, anything clinical. Marina handles these personally, never the AI.

The reply (if it’s out of scope but coachable)

[Name],

Thank you for trusting me with this. What you’re asking about is real, important, and not something I can answer well in an email — or from a quiz. It’s a conversation.

If you want to think it through with me 1:1, the Clarity Session is the right place to start. One hour, $497, written follow-up within 48 business hours: [Stripe link].

If it would help to know whether your specific situation is something I work with, just reply with two more sentences about what you’re weighing. I’ll tell you honestly if it’s in my scope or if I’d refer you to someone better suited.

— Marina

The reply (if it’s clinical — depression, trauma, abuse, self-harm)

[Name],

What you’re describing belongs in the room with a licensed therapist before it belongs in a coaching conversation. I want to say that not to brush you off — but because the right kind of help here matters more than what I do.

Two resources, depending on where you are:

— Psychology Today’s therapist directory for ongoing support.
— If this is urgent or you’re in crisis: 988 in the US (Suicide and Crisis Lifeline), or your local emergency line.

Once you have that support, if you want to come back and work on the dating side — I’ll be here. The coaching part comes after. It’s alongside the therapy, not instead of it.

— Marina

What to customize: use the right version. If anything in their email signals self-harm or imminent danger — use the second version, immediately, no upsell, no exception.

The Marina-voice rules for replies

  1. Always start with their name on its own line.
  2. Always reference one specific thing they said in your first sentence (not just “thanks for reaching out”).
  3. Never use therapy-speak (“I hear you,” “I see you,” “valid”).
  4. Never promise outcomes (“you’ll meet someone”).
  5. End with the Marina sign-off: — Marina. Em-dash, lowercase “m”, no comma.
  6. Never send the same template twice in a row to the same person. If they reply again, write something new.
  7. If you’re unsure which template to use, default to Template 4 (no pressure). It’s never wrong.
